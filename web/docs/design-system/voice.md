# Voice

TTV writes the way a good member of the community talks: plainly, warmly,
without overselling. Words are part of the interface, so they follow rules.
Each rule has examples on `/style-guide/voice`.

## Community voice

TTV helps grow tech communities across Africa with the people in them. Write
about what people build, learn and do together. Never rescue framing
("empowering underserved youth"), and never soften the Africa focus to avoid
it: say what people do, at its real size.

## Plain words

Short sentences, common words, active voice, for someone reading on a phone
between other things, perhaps in their second language. Sentence case for
everything. No capitals for emphasis, no eyebrows, no exclamation marks to
sound friendly.

## Actions

A button says what will happen ("Send request", "Not this month"); a link
says where it goes ("Find a mentor"). Name what an action acts on when it
cannot be undone ("Remove request"). Never "Submit", "OK" or "Click here".

## Errors and empty lists

Say what happened, whose problem it is, and what to do next. Never blame the
reader; never leave them at a dead end.

- "We couldn't send your request. Your note is saved: check your connection and send it again."
- "Enter an email address, like wanjiku@example.com."
- "No requests yet. When you ask a mentor for time, it waits here until they answer."

## Tembo

The AI is Tembo everywhere: "Ask Tembo", "@tembo", never "Ask AI". It has its
own mark, carries the AI badge wherever it speaks, says what it looked
through, cites its sources, and never pretends to be a person.

## Names, numbers, dates

- People by their name as they wrote it; the role badge says the role.
- Numbers with what they count ("3 unread messages"); "1 person", "2 people".
- Dates readable at a glance ("3 Nov", "Today, 14:05").
- Never a pronoun the person has not given; write around it ("David will see it in Mentoring").
- Examples use realistic, fictional names (the shared fixtures in
  `src/lib/platform/fixtures.ts`) and say they are fictional. Never a real
  member's name or record.

## Honest claims

Only describe what exists. If something is coming, say so plainly or say
nothing. No invented outcomes, statistics or testimonials. "Not enough
evidence" is its own state, never a low score.
